The Living Pattern

Book ✸ Design

Year

2025

Client

Typography II

Tools

InDesign, Illustrator

Skills

Pacing, Layout Design, Typographic Systems

This book design explores how typography can function as a visual argument, reinterpreting two contrasting art historical texts into distinct yet interconnected typographic systems.

The Lesser Arts by William Morris and Questions of Style by Alois Riegl present opposing perspectives on ornament, materiality, and modern design. Morris champions craftsmanship and the decorative arts as essential to daily life, while Riegl approaches style through structure, abstraction, and historical analysis. Their differing viewpoints provided me with an engaging framework to investigate how design language reflects ideology and cultural context.

Drawing from the historical movements associated with each author, I created two contrasting typographic systems.

Morris’s text takes shape through an Art Nouveau–inspired style, with fluid, interconnected letterforms that echo ironwork and textiles. Riegl’s writing, on the other hand, is interpreted through the aesthetics of Viennese Secessionism, where structure and geometry define composition. Each system is a creative response to the unique tone and philosophy of its source, while maintaining a thoughtful visual conversation.

✸

This project expanded my appreciation for typography as both expressive and conceptual. Treating type as a visual language, I discovered how form, structure, and ornament can actively shape meaning. The experience strengthened my passion for historically informed design and affirmed the value of using typographic systems to illuminate and enrich complex ideas with clarity and creativity.
