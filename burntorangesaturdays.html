Burnt Orange Saturdays

Interactive Object / 2026

  • Book Arts
  • Material Study
  • Archival Research

For as long as I can remember, Saturdays have been game days: whichever house we were living in, my family gathered every fall weekend to watch Texas Longhorns football. Burnt Orange Saturdays holds that ritual as a shoebox-sized object lined with cow fur, leather, and burnt orange satin, opening into two chambers that can be explored in either order.

Concept

Over eighteen years, my family moved through eight houses, and Longhorns football stayed constant, a source of pride and love we could always return to. The box makes that memory physical: one chamber holds my family’s story, the other the culture surrounding the team, and the viewer opens, handles, and unfolds them to piece the story together.

The Book

The outer chamber holds a continuous unfolding book. One side pairs sequential childhood photographs with games and family events from my parents’ albums, with burnt orange bringing family members forward across time. The other side shows each house I have lived in beside a map of its town, keeping the history of moving in view as the story of continuity unfolds.

The Ephemera

The inner chamber collects ephemera from internet archives and Longhorn traditions, from fight song sheet music and team legends to mascot photographs and game program covers. Each piece is printed on a textured paper chosen for its content, so the collection feels found rather than produced, like fragments pulled from a family drawer that place our ritual within the larger tradition.
